Long-context: 200 pages, one prompt.

Claude can hold 200,000+ tokens in context — roughly 500 pages — and reason across all of it at once. Most people never use this; they paste short snippets when they could paste whole books. This lesson covers the three workflows where long context changes what's possible — and, just as important, where it starts to break.

Step 1 of 50% complete
The mental model

Long context isn't a bigger chat — it's a different analysis

When Claude can see your entire 80-page contract, your full quarterly report, or a whole small codebase at once, the questions you can ask change. The instinct most people have is also the mistake.

Predict first

You need Claude to analyze an 80-page report. What do most people get wrong?

Three things it unlocks

Stop summarizing. Let it see the whole thing.

① Multi-document synthesis
Upload several documents and ask comparative questions across all of them at once.
"Here are 5 competitor quarterly reports — compare their AI strategies and where each is investing."
② Full-document interrogation
Paste one long document and ask many specific questions without re-pasting.
"Find every clause that limits our liability. Quote them with section numbers."
③ Codebase understanding
Load a small codebase (under ~100 files) and ask architectural questions.
"Walk me through how authentication flows from request to response."

The discipline

Trust the analysis, verify the citation

Long-context analysis is powerful, but it can occasionally invent a section number or misquote a line buried in hundreds of pages.

The call

Claude reviews your contract and quotes a liability cap "in §7.3." Before you act on it, what do you do?

What you can do now

  • Paste entire documents instead of summarizing them down first
  • Synthesize patterns across several documents in one session
  • Interrogate a long contract, report, or paper with targeted questions
  • Know the boundary — when to expect general patterns or reach for Claude Code
  • Verify cited quotes and section numbers against the source

Your move: feed it your biggest document

Find a document you've been meaning to read carefully — a contract, a long report, a research paper, a manual. Paste the whole thing into Claude and ask ten specific questions that would have taken an hour to answer by hand. Then verify the two or three answers you'd actually act on.
