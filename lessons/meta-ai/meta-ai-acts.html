Meta AI stopped waiting for you to ask: it acts now.

Two weeks after shipping the Muse Spark 1.1 model, Meta flipped the switch on what it was built for. Starting July 24, 2026, Meta AI in the app and on meta.ai can make plans, connect to your email and calendar, run recurring tasks, research topics on its own, build slides — and follow through without being re-prompted. This is Meta's version of the agent race everyone else is running. Here's the tour, and the one decision to make before you connect anything.

What you can now hand off

The pattern across every new feature: you describe the outcome once, and Meta AI owns the follow-through. Meta's own launch examples, translated to the everyday version:

You say, once
Meta AI does, ongoing
"Brief me every morning at 7"
Pulls your calendar, flags double-bookings and changed plans, and delivers a summary at your chosen time, daily.
"I'm renovating my kitchen"
Learns your style, scouts Marketplace for fixtures in budget, and sends a mood board of how it fits together.
"Get me ready for a half marathon"
Builds a week-by-week training schedule around your availability and re-sends your plan every Monday morning.
"Plan a birthday dinner"
Finds restaurants, checks your calendar for a night that works, and suggests options.
"Track sneaker drops" / "weekly meal plan"
Recurring tasks: set up once, then updates arrive on schedule without another prompt.

Research that ends in slides

Ask Meta AI to dig into a topic and it now runs a proper deep dive — synthesizing the open web, research papers, and (this is the Meta-flavored part) what creators and communities post across Meta's own apps. When it's done, one more ask turns the findings into generated slides.

The detail we like most: you can steer it mid-job. While it assembles a report or deck, tell it to shift focus, change the tone, or cut a section, and it course-corrects live instead of starting over. Everything it makes lands in one place where you can revisit, build on, or share it.

How to check if you have it

Open the Meta AI app or meta.ai and ask for something with follow-through built in: "Give me a daily briefing at 8am" or "Plan my sister's birthday dinner next month." If it offers to set up the recurring task or asks to connect your calendar, you're in the rollout. If it just answers once, you're not yet — it began July 24 in select markets, with more countries and surfaces (including WhatsApp) promised in the coming weeks.

The honest part

"Handles things for you" means "sees things about you." The daily briefing needs your calendar. Inbox awareness needs your email. The shopping features work because Meta AI watches what you browse and like across Meta's apps — and Meta is a company whose business is knowing what you'll buy. None of that is hidden, but connecting email and calendar to Meta is a bigger data decision than chatting with an assistant. Read Privacy on Meta AI — what's shared & what to lock down first, use Incognito chats for anything you don't want informing the profile, and note the rollout is "select markets" — feature behavior may shift while it's landing.

Meta AI's agent features — quick answers

What exactly rolled out on July 24?
Agentic features powered by Muse Spark 1.1: plans with follow-through, email and calendar connections, daily briefings, recurring tasks, research deep dives, slide generation, and mid-job steering — in the Meta AI app and meta.ai, select markets first.
Is it free?
Yes — these features are part of Meta AI, not a paid tier.
Will it work in WhatsApp?
Meta says WhatsApp and other surfaces are coming "in the coming weeks" after the July 24 start. Today it's the Meta AI app and meta.ai.
How is this different from ChatGPT's or Gemini's agents?
Same race, different home turf. ChatGPT's agent features center on work documents and the browser; Gemini's on Google's office suite. Meta AI's edge is the social graph: Marketplace scouting, creator-and-community research, and living inside apps you already open daily. Its gap: it can't touch your spreadsheets.
What model powers this?
Muse Spark 1.1 — Meta Superintelligence Labs' agentic model. The full story on the model itself is in Muse Spark 1.1, explained.
