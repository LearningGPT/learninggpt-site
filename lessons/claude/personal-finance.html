Claude can now read your bank accounts. Here's how to use it well.

On November 5, 2026, Anthropic launched Personal Finance in Claude: connect your bank and card accounts, then ask about your money in your own words — “where did it all go this month?”, “what's due before payday?”, “anything look off?” It's genuinely useful, and it's also the most sensitive data most people will ever hand an AI. This lesson covers what it does, exactly what it can see, what it can't do, and a safe first ten minutes.

Who has it right now

Beta, rolling out starting the week of November 5, 2026, on the Pro, Max, and Ultra plans, US only. Not on Free, not outside the US yet. You'll know it's reached you when Personal Finance appears in Claude's sidebar. If it isn't there, there's nothing to switch on — it just hasn't arrived.

01 What it actually does

Instead of opening four banking apps and doing the math yourself, you ask Claude. It reads the balances and transactions from the accounts you connected and answers in everyday language, explaining any term you don't know. Anthropic's own walkthrough shows the kind of conversation it's built for:

  • “Where did my money go this month?” — it totals your spending across accounts and sorts it by category.
  • “Is that more than usual?” — it compares with previous months and points out what changed (in Anthropic's example, a membership renewal).
  • “What's left before payday?” — it lists payments still coming out of checking and what you'll have left.
  • “Does anything look off?” — it scans for the easy-to-miss stuff: a charge that hit twice, an unexpected fee, a bill that's crept up all year.
  • “What's an APR, and what has mine cost me this year?” — it explains the term, then adds up the interest you've actually been charged.

It can also build things you keep adjusting — a weekly spending chart, a calendar of what's due, or a small dashboard of the numbers you care about.

02 What it can see — and what it can't do

This is the section to read twice. Per Anthropic's announcement:

  • Read-only. Personal Finance can't move, manage, or invest your money. No transfers, no payments.
  • Your bank password stays with your bank. You connect by signing in to your bank yourself through Plaid, a service that links apps to banks; that's where you approve Claude's read-only access. Claude doesn't ask for or see the password.
  • Only the accounts you pick. For each one: the bank's name; the account's name, type, and last four digits; balances and credit limits; and up to two years of transactions.
  • Hub chats aren't used for training. Conversations inside the Personal Finance hub aren't used to train Anthropic's models. Money talk you have outside the hub follows your normal Claude privacy settings — so keep finance questions in the hub.
  • You can unlink anytime, and Claude then can no longer see that account's data.
  • No ads, no products to sell you. Anthropic says it has no sponsored recommendations and doesn't offer its own credit cards, loans, or savings accounts.
It's not an advisor, and it can be wrong. Anthropic says plainly that Personal Finance isn't designed to give investment advice and that Claude can make mistakes. The habit that protects you: whenever a number matters, ask “which transactions make up that number?” and check anything important against your bank. A confident total is still a total you should verify — the same discipline as spotting hallucinations.

03 A safe first ten minutes

Minute 1 — Open the hub
Click Personal Finance in Claude's sidebar. Do your money questions here, not in a regular chat, so the no-training rule for hub conversations applies.
Minutes 2–4 — Connect ONE account
Start with your main checking account, not everything. You'll sign in on your bank's page via Plaid. If anything asks you to type your bank password into a chat box, stop — that isn't how this works.
Minutes 5–7 — Ask one real question
“Where did my money go last month, by category?” Then follow up: “Which transactions are in the food category?” Spot-check two or three against your bank app.
Minutes 8–10 — Hunt for leaks
“Show me recurring charges and anything that charged twice or went up this year.” Forgotten subscriptions and creeping bills are where this pays for itself fastest.

Only after it's earned your trust on one account should you add cards or savings. More connected accounts means better answers — and more of your financial life in one place.

04 The scam angle: tell your family how this really works

Whenever a big-name AI feature touches bank accounts, scammers follow within weeks with fake “connect your bank to Claude” messages. Knowing the real flow makes the fakes easy to spot:

  • The real feature lives inside the Claude app or claude.ai, in the sidebar. It doesn't arrive as a text message, email link, or phone call.
  • You sign in on your bank's own login through Plaid. Nobody from “Claude support” will ever need your password, a one-time code, or remote access to your computer.
  • It's read-only. Anything claiming Claude needs to “move funds to verify your account” is a scam, full stop.

If you help an older parent with technology, this is worth a two-minute conversation. Our online banking, done safely lesson in the AI for Seniors track covers the broader playbook.

05 Should you turn it on?

If you already pay for Claude in the US and you don't have a budgeting app you actually open, it's worth trying on one account — the “what's due before payday” and “anything look off” questions are the strongest use. If you're uneasy about any AI seeing two years of transactions, that's a reasonable call too: the feature is optional, and nothing else in Claude changes if you skip it. Either way, it doesn't replace a licensed professional for the big decisions — Anthropic itself says those are best handled with the professionals and regulated firms you trust.
