The reframe that unlocks everything

If you treat Copilot like ChatGPT — generic prompt, generic draft, copy-paste — you're using 10% of what you pay for. Copilot in Outlook is inbox-aware: it can see your messages, calendar, contacts, and past replies. Every workflow below is built on that.

01 · Draft from a prompt

Basic
Where: New email → "Draft with Copilot" (pen + sparkle icon, above the message body). Replying? It reads the thread automatically.

The skill is the prompt. Start with the weak version and sharpen it — tap each piece Copilot needs:

Your promptReply to Sarah about the budget.
Draft quality:
generic template
What Copilot hands backHi Sarah — I'm on board with the Q3 proposal in principle. One tweak: can we push sign-off to Oct 15 so I can pull final numbers? Before we lock it, I'd love a quick 30-minute review call — does Thursday work?
Gotcha: Copilot defaults to verbose corporate tone. Always add a word count and a voice note ("conversational, skip the throat-clearing") or you get email muzak.
~5 min → ~30 sec per draft · 30–60 min/day for heavy email

02 · Summarize a thread in one click

Intermediate
Where: Open a thread → "Summary by Copilot" banner at the top of the reading pane (appears on multi-reply threads).

It reads the whole back-and-forth and returns decisions, open questions, action items, and who's waiting on what. Then you can ask follow-ups right in the summary pane:

Follow-ups that workWhat's the most recent position from Finance? What action items are still open, and who owns them? Did anyone explicitly disagree — and why?

03 · Replies that pull from your other emails

Advanced
Where: Draft with Copilot — but with a prompt that references info outside the current thread. This is what separates it from a generic chatbot.

A client asks, "what discount did you offer last quarter?" Instead of digging through Sent Items:

The Copilot moveReply to this client. Find the discount percentage I offered them last quarter from my sent emails and include it. Short — just the number plus a friendly note.

Copilot opens Draft mode, searches your sent items, finds the figure, and writes it into the reply. Five minutes → 30 seconds. But know its reach:

04 · Inbox-wide briefings

Power user
Where: Copilot chat panel (sidebar icon or the standalone M365 Copilot app) — the chat surface, not the drafting one.

This is the "wait, you can do that?" move: query your whole inbox across folders, senders, and time, and get a synthesized briefing. The Monday-morning prompt:

Monday-morning briefingSummarize emails from over the weekend. Group by: (1) needs my reply this week, (2) FYI only, (3) probably ignorable. For group 1, note what each is asking for.

Reading that takes 90 seconds; reading the 40 emails takes 25 minutes. Same info, 17× faster — this one workflow justifies the license.

05 · Rewrite your own drafts

Underused
Where: Highlight your text → "Rewrite with Copilot" (or "Coaching") → pick the change: shorter, more direct, more diplomatic, etc.

Write the draft yourself, then have Copilot adjust tone or length. Rewrite changes it; Coaching just suggests what to change so you learn the patterns.

The gotcha

You ask "make it shorter" and Copilot cuts 200 words to 80 — dropping your main ask. Best move?

Stay honest — what it still can't do
  • Occasionally hallucinates context, especially in summaries — sanity-check key details before sending.
  • Mobile is weaker than desktop/web; many features are desktop-only.
  • Won't search SharePoint, Teams chats, or OneDrive without connectors your admin enables.
  • Can't auto-send — every draft needs your click. (A feature, not a bug.)
  • Deep/archived-folder search is hit-or-miss; recent inbox is best.
✓

Lesson complete

Five workflows, one routine. Stack them and a 2-hour inbox becomes about 20 minutes.

Your Monday-morning experiment

  1. Open Copilot chat first — run the Monday briefing (WF4) before opening your inbox.
  2. For each long thread, summarize before reading (WF2).
  3. Draft replies with a specific, constrained prompt (WF1) — use cross-inbox context where needed (WF3).
  4. Rewrite the 2–3 most sensitive ones before sending (WF5).
  5. Time it, and compare to a normal Monday. Most people see 50–70% off on the first try.
What you can do now

Write constrained prompts that draft in 30 seconds, summarize before reading, pull cross-inbox context, generate full-inbox briefings, and refine tone — and you know where Copilot's edges are.
