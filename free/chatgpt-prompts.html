✍️ Writing that sounds like you

1. First draft of anything

Write a [email / post / message] about [topic] for [audience]. Goal: [what I want]. Keep it clear, warm, and under [N] words. No corporate filler.

2. Match my voice

Here are 2 things I've written. Learn my tone, then write [new thing] the way I would. Don't make it sound like AI.

3. Make it shorter and sharper

Tighten this by 40%. Keep every key point and my voice; cut the padding.

4. Fix the tricky reply

Help me reply to this message. I want to [say no / push back / ask for more time] without burning the relationship. Give me 2 versions.

5. Beat writer's block

I need to write [thing] but I'm stuck. Ask me 3 questions to pull the ideas out of my head, then draft it from my answers.

💼 Work & getting things done

6. Turn a mess into a plan

Here are my scattered notes about [project]. Turn them into a clear action plan: steps in order, owners, and what to do first.

7. Summarize the long thing

Summarize this [document / thread / transcript]: the 3 things that matter, any decisions, and anything that needs me. Bullets only.

8. Prep for the meeting

I have a meeting with [who] about [topic]. Give me a 5-point brief: their likely goals, my key points, and 2 questions to ask.

9. Draft the awkward message

Write a message to [person] about [sensitive issue]. Honest but kind, solution-focused, no blame.

10. Make the decision doc

Help me write a one-page recommendation on [decision]: the options, the tradeoffs, my pick, and why.

🧠 Think & decide better

11. Pressure-test my thinking

Here's my plan: [plan]. Play devil's advocate. What am I missing, what could go wrong, and what would you do differently?

12. Weigh two options

I'm choosing between [A] and [B]. Ask me 3 questions that would actually decide it, then wait for my answers.

13. Second opinion

Explain the strongest case FOR and the strongest case AGAINST [thing I'm leaning toward]. Be fair to both.

14. Find the real problem

Here's the situation: [describe]. Help me figure out the root cause, not just the symptom. Ask questions if you need them.

📚 Learn anything faster

15. Explain it simply

Explain [topic] to me like I'm smart but new to it. Use a plain-English analogy. 3 short paragraphs.

16. The 80/20 of a topic

I want to get useful at [skill] fast. What are the 20% of things that give 80% of the results? Give me a starter plan.

17. Quiz me

Teach me [topic] by asking me one question at a time, checking my answer, and going a little deeper each time.

18. Decode the jargon

Rewrite this [document / email / contract] in plain English, and flag anything I should ask about.

19. Compare and choose

Compare [option A] vs [option B] for someone who cares most about [priority]. Give me a clear recommendation, not a shrug.

🏠 Everyday life

20. Plan the thing

Help me plan [trip / event / week]. Ask me the 3 things you need to know, then give me a simple plan.

21. Meal ideas that fit

Give me 5 dinner ideas that are [quick / healthy / kid-friendly] using mostly [what I have]. Short recipes.

22. Draft the tough personal note

Help me write a heartfelt [thank-you / apology / congratulations] to [person] about [what]. Warm and genuine, not stiff.

23. Untangle a task

I've been putting off [task] because it feels big. Break it into the smallest possible first step, then the next few.

24. Explain it to a kid

My [child / grandchild] asked me [question]. Help me explain it in a way a [age]-year-old would understand and enjoy.

25. The "make this better" catch-all

Here's something I made. Make it clearer and better, keep it mine, and tell me the one change that matters most and why.
