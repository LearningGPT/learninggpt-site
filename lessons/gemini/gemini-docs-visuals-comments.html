Docs gets a design desk — and a comment handler.

On July 28, 2026, Google shipped two Gemini upgrades to Google Docs at once. The first turns Docs into a place that can make its own visuals — images, diagrams, infographics — built from what your document actually says. The second points Gemini at the messiest part of collaboration: the comment thread. Both are rolling out now. Here's what each one does, the prompts to try, and the fine print on who sees it when.

01 Two upgrades, one minute

FeatureWhat it doesWhere you'll find it

Comment workflowsSummarizes and answers questions about comments, adds comments for you, drafts replies, and suggests edits based on feedbackBottom bar or side panel — requires edit access on the doc

02 Visuals that know what your doc says

The point isn't that Docs can generate an image — plenty of tools do that. It's that the visuals are grounded in your document. Ask for “a diagram providing an overview of the proposal at the top of my doc” and Gemini reads the proposal to build it. Existing visuals can be refined in plain language, and you can update several at once — add an infographic to each key section, or restyle every visual to match.

Prompts to try this week

  • “Create a rich infographic visually summarizing my doc” — the fastest way to see what it can do
  • “Add a diagram providing an overview of the proposal at the top of my doc”
  • “Change the aspect ratio to 16:9” — refinement is natural language too
  • “Make the style more aesthetic to match the rest of the document”
  • “Add an infographic to each key section” — batch creation in one ask

03 Gemini works the comment thread

Comment workflows aim at the doc that comes back from review with forty comments. Gemini can now read and act on all of them: summarize what reviewers said, tell you which issues are still unresolved, draft replies grounded in the document, leave comments on your behalf, and — the strongest part — generate suggested edits that address a reviewer's feedback, which you approve before they touch the text. You may also see proactive nudges: a summary offer when you open a heavily commented doc, or a drafted reply when you click into a thread.

The comment prompts worth stealing

  • “Summarize all comments from Sarah” — one reviewer's feedback, digested
  • “Based on the comments, what are the unresolved issues?” — your pre-meeting triage
  • “Reply to the thread confirming that we're approved to launch on April 1”
  • “Add a comment asking Roberta to verify the stats in the launch readiness section”
  • “Rewrite the introduction to address Roberta's feedback” — produces suggested edits you review and apply
Rollout is gradual — up to 15 days for the features to appear, starting July 28, 2026. Visual generation is web only, comment workflows need edit access, and both require Workspace smart features to be on (and, for work accounts, your admin to have Gemini for Workspace enabled). Not seeing it today doesn't mean you won't have it next week.

04 Who gets it

Eligibility

Work: Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus. School: Education Plus and certain education add-ons. Personal: Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. Basic/Starter Workspace tiers and free personal accounts aren't on the July 28 list — for what each Google plan includes, see what Gemini costs.

Zoom out and the direction is clear: Google is making Docs a place where Gemini doesn't just write — it illustrates the doc and manages the review cycle around it. Frequently asked

Gemini's new Docs powers — your questions, answered

What did Google add to Docs on July 28, 2026?
Two Gemini capabilities: visual generation (create and edit images, diagrams, and infographics using your document’s context) and comment workflows (Gemini can summarize comment threads, answer questions about feedback, add comments, draft replies, and suggest edits based on reviewer feedback). Both started a gradual rollout on July 28, 2026.
How do I use the new visuals feature?
In Google Docs on the web, use the bottom bar or the Gemini side panel and ask in plain language — for example “Create a rich infographic visually summarizing my doc” or “Add a diagram providing an overview of the proposal at the top of my doc.” You can refine results the same way: “Change the aspect ratio to 16:9.” It’s currently web-only.
What can Gemini do with comments?
Summarize all comments or just one person’s, surface unresolved issues, insert comments on your behalf, draft contextually grounded replies to open threads, and generate suggested edits that address reviewer feedback — which you review and approve before they apply. You need edit access on the document.
Who gets these features?
Workspace Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, Education Plus, and consumers on Google AI Pro or Ultra, plus certain education add-ons. Admins must have Gemini for Workspace enabled, and users need Workspace smart features turned on.
I don’t see it yet — is something wrong?
Probably not. Google says the rollout is gradual, with up to 15 days for feature visibility from July 28, 2026. If it still doesn’t appear after that, check that smart features are enabled in your settings and that your plan is on the eligibility list.
