The four surfaces

Four ways to let Claude do the work

Each agent is a different place Claude can roll up its sleeves. Here's the whole map:

Coworkyour files & apps
A desktop coworker — reads your files, builds documents, searches your email, runs code on your data.
e.g. "Pull the open action items from 30 days of meeting notes into a Word doc grouped by owner."
Claude Codeyour codebase
An engineering coworker in your terminal — reads the repo, writes code, runs tests, makes commits.
e.g. "Add JSDoc to every function in src/utils, run the tests, show me the diff before committing."
Computer Useyour screen
Claude clicks, types, and navigates your desktop — for apps and legacy systems that have no API.
e.g. "Open the old timesheet app, enter last week's hours, submit, and screenshot the confirmation."
MCPyour other tools
A standard that connects Claude to services — Outlook, Slack, Linear, GitHub, your database — to read and act without copy-paste.
e.g. "Pull my stale Linear P0s and draft Slack status updates — don't post, let me review."

Why it matters

The shift most people haven't noticed yet

Most AI tools are chat windows: they hand you text, and you do the work of pasting it where it belongs. The agent layer flips that — Claude does the work in your real environment and hands back a finished result.

The practical revolution

"AI gives me text I have to paste" → "AI delivers a finished result." That's the whole shift. Everything else in this track is learning to use it well — and to keep a hand on the wheel where it counts.

You don't have to learn all four at once. Pick the one that maps to where your time goes — files and docs (Cowork), code (Claude Code), no-API apps (Computer Use), or connected tools (MCP) — and start there.
