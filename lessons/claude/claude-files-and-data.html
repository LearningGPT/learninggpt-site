Files & data: reading at machine scale.

Claude's long context isn't a spec-sheet number — it's the ability to drop a pile of documents into one conversation and ask questions across all of them at once. That changes what 'reading' means for your work.

01 The multi-document move

One document is table stakes. The real unlock is several at once: the contract AND the proposal AND the email thread — then: "Where do these disagree? What did we promise in the proposal that the contract doesn't capture?" Cross-document contradiction hunting is tedious, error-prone human work and nearly free machine work. It alone justifies the habit. (How the context window makes this possible — and its limits: the long-context lesson.)

02 The document-pile playbook

Orient: "What did I give you? Summarize each document in a line and note anything that seems missing or truncated." Catches upload problems before they poison answers.
Interrogate: specific beats general — "What are the termination conditions?" outperforms "summarize the contract." Ask the questions you'd ask a paralegal who'd read everything.
Locate: for anything that matters, "Quote the exact passage that says this, and tell me which document and where." Claude quoting its evidence is your verification shortcut — check the quote, not the whole file.
Extract: "Build a table: every date-bound obligation across these documents — who owes what, to whom, by when." Documents in, structure out.

03 Spreadsheets and data files

Claude reads data files and its analysis tool can compute over them — totals, trends, breakdowns — with the same conversational ladder as any analysis: orient, verify one anchor number you already know, then probe. For heavy analytics (forecasting, segmentation), be honest about tool fit: Claude for the reasoning about the numbers, a dedicated analysis environment for industrial-strength number crunching. Judgment about data is Claude's lane, and it's the rarer skill anyway.

The honest limits

Enormous piles can exceed even a large context — split by theme and synthesize the summaries. Scanned documents read imperfectly — spot-check anything critical against the original page. And the standing rule for sensitive files: know your plan's data policy before uploading other people's information. Two minutes of hygiene beats one apology.

04 Where this pays first

The workflows that convert skeptics: contract review before signing ("what should worry me?"), vendor-proposal comparison (three quotes, one table), policy-manual Q&A ("what does our handbook actually say about this?"), and the annual pile-of-statements tax-time excavation. Pick whichever one is currently on your desk.
