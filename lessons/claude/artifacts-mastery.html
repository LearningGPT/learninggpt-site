Claude Projects: your personal research assistant.

Projects is the Claude feature most people have never opened — and it changes everything. A Project is a persistent workspace where Claude carries your context, your knowledge base, and your style into every conversation. This lesson sets up three: a research assistant, a writing partner, and a code reviewer — and the instruction traps that quietly make a Project worse.

Step 1 of 50% complete
The mental model

Chat is amnesiac. A Project remembers.

A normal Claude chat starts fresh every time. A Project keeps your custom instructions, your uploaded knowledge base (docs, PDFs, transcripts), and the style you've set — so every new conversation inside it starts with all of that already loaded.

Predict first

You start every Claude chat by pasting the same three paragraphs about your industry and goals. What should you do instead?

Three Projects worth building

Same setup, three jobs

Every Project is the same recipe — a name, custom instructions, and a knowledge base. Here are three high-leverage ones, with the one trap each:

① Research Assistant
Knows your field and your past notes; every chat opens with that context.
Instructions: "Bias toward evidence, flag your confidence, link sources, never invent stats." · Upload: background docs, papers, notes.
Trap: don't upload sensitive data (customer info, contracts) without an enterprise data agreement.
② Writing Coach
Knows your voice from real samples; gives feedback that keeps you sounding like you.
Instructions: "Feedback on clarity, voice, structure; never rewrite without permission." · Upload: 3–5 of your own writing samples.
Trap: don't have it write FOR you in your voice — that flattens you. Use it for feedback on your writing.
③ Code Reviewer
Knows your conventions, style guide, and review priorities.
Instructions: "Review for correctness, readability, testability, performance; cite lines; suggest, don't rewrite." · Upload: style guide, exemplar PRs, test patterns.
Trap: not a substitute for human review on critical code — a faster first pass, not the final word.
The rule of thumb

If you find yourself re-explaining the same context more than twice, that topic should be a Project. Setup is one-time; the payoff is every future conversation on it.
