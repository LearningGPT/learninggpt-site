Claude Mastery Free ~7 min read Updated · August 2026

Anthropic just opened its own school. Here’s the map.

Claude Academy went live on August 20, 2026 at academy.claude.com — twenty-two free courses, plus a large library of tutorials and use cases. It is genuinely good. It is also written by the company that sells Claude, and that shapes what it teaches. This lesson is the honest tour: what is in there, which door to walk through, and where the free vendor course runs out.

01 What is actually on the shelf

Three kinds of material, and confusing them is the fastest way to waste an evening.

  • Courses (22 of them). Sequenced, multi-lesson, mostly with a quiz or a certificate at the end. This is the part that behaves like a course.
  • Tutorials (~120). Short, task-shaped, no sequence. Closer to documentation with a friendlier voice — useful when you already know the name of the thing you are stuck on.
  • Use cases (~150). Worked examples of a job someone did with Claude. Best read as prompt inspiration, not instruction.

The courses are also bundled into collections — AI Fluency, Claude for You, Claude for Work, Build with Claude, and separate ones for K-12, nonprofits, and small business — and there are product tracks for Claude, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, the Claude Platform, and Claude Tag.

02 Pick your door

Never used Claude. Start with Claude 101, then come back and do the fluency course. Do it the other way around and the abstractions have nothing to stick to.
Used it, plateaued. Go straight to AI Fluency Framework: Foundations. It is the one that changes how you work rather than what buttons you press.
Rolling it out to a team. The Claude for Work collection, plus whichever role course fits — educators, K-12 educators, students, nonprofits, small businesses, or the train-the-trainer track if you are the one teaching it.
Building on it. Claude Platform 101 and Building with the Claude API, then Agent Skills, Model Context Protocol, and Subagents. There are also courses for running Claude through Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud’s Vertex AI.

03 The 4D framework, in one paragraph

The fluency courses all hang off the same four-part idea, and it is worth knowing even if you never open the course. Delegation is deciding what to hand to the AI at all. Description is saying what you want clearly enough to get it. Discernment is judging what came back — the step most people skip. Diligence is owning the output once you send it, because your name is on it and not Claude’s. The Foundations course walks a dozen-plus short lessons through those four, including a closer look at each one and a certificate of completion at the end.

The one that matters

If you take nothing else: Discernment is the skill that separates people who get value from AI from people who get plausible-sounding slop. Delegation and Description are learnable in an afternoon. Judging output is a habit you build over months, on work you actually understand.

04 What a vendor course cannot teach you

This is not a knock on the material — it is a structural limit. Anthropic’s curriculum is built to make you good at Claude. It is not built to answer these:

  • Should this task go to Claude at all? Some jobs belong in Copilot because your data lives in Microsoft 365. Some belong in a spreadsheet. A Claude course will not tell you that.
  • What does the alternative cost? Pricing comparisons across vendors are nobody’s job but yours. Ours is here.
  • What is your organisation’s risk posture? Retention, watermarking, connector permissions — the vendor documents its own behaviour honestly, but the policy call is local.
A certificate of completion is a record that you finished the material. It is not an assessed credential, and no hiring manager has ever been moved by one on its own. What moves people is a folder of real work you produced faster and better than you could have before. Build the folder.

05 A two-week plan that actually finishes

Do this, not “work through the site”

  • Week 1, one sitting: Claude 101 if you are new, otherwise AI Fluency Framework: Foundations. Finish it. Do not browse.
  • Week 1, the rest: apply the 4D loop to one real recurring task at work. One task. Same task every day.
  • Week 2, one sitting: the product course closest to how you actually work — Cowork if you live in files, Claude Code if you live in a terminal.
  • Week 2, the rest: pick the three tutorials that map to the friction you hit in week 1. Skip the other 117.
  • End of week 2: write down what Claude is now reliably better than you at, and what it is still worse at. That list is the actual deliverable.

Free training from the vendor is a gift — take it. Just take it with the thing a vendor cannot hand you: a view across the tools, and a reason to pick one.

Questions people ask

Claude Academy, answered

Is Claude Academy free?
Yes. Anthropic describes Claude Academy as free courses, tutorials, and use cases, published at academy.claude.com. It went live on August 20, 2026. Anthropic is the vendor of Claude, so treat the material as excellent product training and thin on the question of when a different tool would serve you better.
How many courses does Claude Academy have?
Twenty-two courses as of August 25, 2026, alongside roughly 120 tutorials and about 150 use-case write-ups. The courses are grouped into collections such as AI Fluency, Claude for You, Claude for Work, Build with Claude, and dedicated collections for K-12, nonprofits, and small business.
What is the 4D framework?
It is the spine of Anthropic's AI Fluency Framework: Foundations course, and it stands for Delegation, Description, Discernment, and Diligence. Delegation is deciding what to hand over, Description is saying what you want well, Discernment is judging what comes back, and Diligence is owning the result. The course runs a dozen-plus short lessons and ends with a certificate of completion.
Which course should I start with?
If you have never used Claude, start with Claude 101. If you want the thinking habits rather than the button-clicking, start with AI Fluency Framework: Foundations. If you are rolling Claude out to a team, the Claude for Work collection and the role-specific fluency courses for educators, nonprofits, and small businesses are the closer fit.
Does Claude Academy cover Cowork and Claude Code?
Yes. The Academy has product tracks for Claude, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, the Claude Platform, and Claude Tag, plus courses including Introduction to Claude Cowork, Claude Code 101, Claude Code in Action, Introduction to Agent Skills, Introduction to Model Context Protocol, and Introduction to Subagents.
Does finishing a course prove anything to an employer?
Be careful here. Several courses issue a certificate of completion, which records that you finished the material. That is not the same as an assessed credential, and no vendor course substitutes for showing the work you did with the tool. Use the certificate as a prompt to build a portfolio of real outputs, not as the outcome itself.
