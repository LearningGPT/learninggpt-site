Writing with Claude: the partner, not the ghostwriter.

Ask around: people who use several AIs almost always route their serious writing to Claude. This lesson is the full workflow — from messy notes to final draft — with your voice intact at the end.

01 Zero-draft from your mess

Dump everything — fragments, bullets, a rambling voice-memo transcript — and ask: "Turn this into a rough first draft. Keep my phrasings where they're strong; flag the gaps you had to bridge." That flag request matters: Claude will tell you where it guessed, which is exactly where your attention should go on the first read.

02 Teach it your voice once

Give Claude three to five samples of your best writing: "Describe this voice precisely — rhythm, word choices, what it never does — then keep that description as our style guide." Save the guide in a Project so every future draft starts from it. Claude's voice-matching is unusually faithful, which cuts both ways: give it your good writing, not your rushed writing, or it will faithfully reproduce your rush.

03 Edit by dimension, not by vibes

You wantSay
Shorter"Cut a third. Protect the argument in paragraph two."
Clearer structure"Re-outline this — what order SHOULD these points be in? Then restructure."
Different energy"Three versions of the opening: warm, direct, provocative."
Real polish"Line-edit pass: tighten every sentence, kill every filler word, keep my voice."

04 The tough-editor pass

Use the pushback

Claude's willingness to disagree is a writing tool: "Be a hard-to-impress editor. Where's the argument weak? What will a skeptical reader think at each section? What should be cut entirely?" Then — this is the discipline — make it defend your favorite part: "Argue that the anecdote should go." If your favorite survives an honest attack, keep it. If not, you needed to know.

05 The line that stays yours

Same rule as every track: writing that IS the relationship — the apology, the condolence, the thank-you that must cost something — you draft. Claude can check clarity; the words should be yours. Everything else: draft fast with the machine, edit like the editor you now permanently employ.

06 One thing that changed under all of this

Since 2 August 2026, supported Claude models embed an invisible watermark in the text they generate and signed provenance metadata in the files they produce — worldwide, with no opt-out. It does not change the writing, and for most drafting work it changes nothing about how you should work. But if you write in a context where AI assistance has to be declared, you should know it is there and know what a detected mark does and does not prove. Claude now marks everything it writes covers it in full.
