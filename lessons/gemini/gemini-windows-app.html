Gemini moves onto the Windows desktop — one keystroke from anything.

On September 10, 2026, Google shipped a native Gemini app for Windows 10 and 11. It's a download, not a website: press Alt + Space and Gemini floats over whatever you were doing, reads from your Gmail and Drive when you ask it to, and makes images with Nano Banana without a browser tab in sight. Here's the five-minute setup, the three jobs it's actually good for on day one, the plan fine print Google buried in footnotes, and the one shortcut collision to fix before it annoys you.

01 Five-minute setup

Download it from gemini.google/desktop, sign in with the Google account you already use for Gemini, and that's it. Google describes the app as lightweight and quiet — it sits in the background waiting for the shortcut rather than sitting on your CPU. It's available globally, today, on Windows 10 and Windows 11.

Alt + Space is a crowded shortcut on Windows. The Copilot app uses it for its own quick view, PowerToys Run defaults to it, and some window managers have claimed it for years. If a different panel pops up when you press it, that's why — rebind one of them before you conclude the Gemini app “doesn't work.”

03 The fine print Google put in footnotes

Read this before you demo it to your boss

The app is free. Google says it works with personal Google accounts, Workspace Individual, and every Google Workspace customer. But two headline features — Gemini Spark and Gemini Omni video — carry a footnote in Google's own announcement, which in practice means they follow your plan, not the app. If you're on a free account, expect the overlay, the Google-app drafting, and Nano Banana; expect Spark and Omni to be gated the way they are on the web. For the plan-by-plan breakdown, see what Gemini costs.

Work accounts: the app is on by default for organizations that already have the Gemini app enabled, controlled by the same Generative AI settings in the Workspace Admin console. There's no per-user toggle. If your admin has Gemini switched off, installing the Windows app won't switch it on — and if your admin has it on, they should know it just became a desktop install. Both sides of that are covered in Workspace governance.

What it isn't yet: Google calls this “just the beginning” and promises more native desktop capabilities over time. Today it's the Gemini app in a native shell with a system-wide shortcut. It doesn't control your PC, click through Windows apps, or read what's on screen unless you paste it in. Treat claims beyond that as rumor until Google ships them.

04 Gemini on Windows vs. Copilot on Windows

If you're a Windows user, you now have two AI assistants a keystroke away, and the useful way to think about them is whose apps they can see, not which model is smarter this month. Copilot is built into Windows and reads Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, and OneDrive. The Gemini app is a download and reads Gmail, Drive, Docs, and Sheets. Most people's work lives mostly in one camp — pick the assistant that matches the camp, and use the other one for general questions. Shops that run Microsoft 365 with a Google side-door (or vice versa) will end up with both installed, which is fine; just settle the shortcut question from section 01 first. Frequently asked

The Gemini app for Windows — your questions, answered

When did the Gemini app for Windows launch?
Google announced the Gemini desktop app for Windows on September 10, 2026, and the Workspace Updates blog confirmed it on September 11. It runs on Windows 10 and 11, is available globally, and downloads from gemini.google/desktop.
What does Alt + Space do?
Alt + Space opens Gemini as an overlay on top of whatever you are working on, for quick asks like fact-checking a paragraph or brainstorming titles. Close it and you are back where you were. If you already use Alt + Space for another launcher or assistant, one of them will need a different shortcut.
Is the Windows app free?
The app itself is free and works with personal Google accounts, Workspace Individual, and all Google Workspace customers. Some features inside it — Gemini Spark and Gemini Omni video — are footnoted by Google as plan-dependent, so expect those to follow your AI Pro or Ultra status rather than the app.
Can it read my Gmail and Google Drive?
Yes. Google's example is asking Gemini to draft a project summary using information pulled directly from Gmail and Drive. That is the same Google-app connection the Gemini app already offers on the web; the Windows app puts it a keystroke away.
I have a work account — do I need to ask IT?
Google says the app is on by default for organizations that already have the Gemini app enabled, and it follows the Generative AI settings in the Workspace Admin console. There is no end-user setting. If Gemini is turned off for your organization, the Windows app will not change that.
How is this different from the Copilot app on Windows?
Copilot is built into Windows and connects to Microsoft 365; the Gemini app is a download that connects to Google apps. If your work lives in Gmail, Drive, Docs, and Sheets, the Gemini app is the one that can see it. If it lives in Outlook, Teams, and SharePoint, Copilot is. Many people will end up with both.
