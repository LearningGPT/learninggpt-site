💵 Quotes, estimates & getting paid

1. Turn rough notes into a clean quote

Turn these job notes into a professional estimate for a customer: [paste notes]. Group it by labor, materials, and any options. Friendly, clear, no jargon.

2. Explain the price so it sticks

A customer thinks my quote for [job] is high. Write a short, respectful reply that explains what the price includes and why it's worth it — without sounding defensive.

3. Chase an unpaid invoice

Write a firm but friendly message to a customer whose invoice for [job] is [X] days overdue. Keep the relationship, but be clear about payment.

4. Write a change order

The customer added [extra work] mid-job. Write a clear change-order note: what's changing, the added cost, and that I need their OK before I proceed.

📱 Customer messages that win the job

5. Reply to a new lead in seconds

Write a quick, professional reply to a new customer asking about [service]. Thank them, ask the 2 questions I need to quote it, and offer to come take a look.

6. The "running late" text

Write a short, polite text telling a customer I'm running about [X] minutes behind and when I'll be there. No excuses, just respect for their time.

7. Cool down an unhappy customer

A customer is upset about [issue]. Write a calm reply that hears them out, takes responsibility where fair, and offers a clear fix.

8. Ask for the review

Write a friendly text asking a happy customer to leave a Google review, with a simple line they could copy if they're not sure what to say.

🧰 On the job

9. Explain a code or spec in plain English

Explain what [code/requirement] means in plain language, and what I need to do to meet it on a typical [type] job. (Then always double-check against local code.)

10. Build a troubleshooting checklist

Give me a step-by-step troubleshooting checklist for [problem] on a [system/unit], from most likely cause to least. Safety first.

11. Materials list from a job

Here's the job: [describe]. List the materials and rough quantities I'll need so I don't make a second supply run.

12. A quick safety toolbox talk

Write a 5-minute toolbox-talk script for my crew about [hazard/task] — clear, practical, and not preachy.

📣 Getting more work

13. A post that gets calls

Write a Facebook post for my [trade] business promoting [service/offer] to local homeowners. Friendly, trustworthy, with a clear "call or message me."

14. Fix up my business page

Write a short, warm "About" section for my [trade] business. I've been doing this [X] years, I serve [area], and what sets me apart is [thing].

15. A job post to hire help

Write a clear job ad to hire a [role] for my [trade] business in [area]: what the work is, what I'm looking for, and how to apply. No fluff.

16. Photo into a post

I just finished a [job]. Here's what I did: [describe]. Write a short before/after style post I can put with the photos.

🗓️ Planning & paperwork

17. Lay out the week

Here are my jobs and rough drive times this week: [list]. Help me order them by day so I'm not crossing town twice, with buffer for overruns.

18. Break down a big job

Break this big job into phases and a rough day-by-day plan: [describe job]. Flag anything that needs an inspection or a second trade.

19. Prep for the site visit

I'm going to quote a [job type]. Give me a checklist of what to measure, photograph, and ask the customer so I can quote it accurately the first time.

20. Draft a simple agreement

Outline a plain-English work agreement for a [job]: scope, price, timeline, payment schedule, and what's not included. (Then have it reviewed before using.)

🧠 Learn & everyday

21. Understand a new product fast

Explain how [product/tool] works and when I'd use it on a job, like you're telling another tradesperson — no marketing talk.

22. Decode the manual

Here's a section of an install manual: [paste]. Put it in plain steps I can follow on site.

23. Talk it out, get it typed

I'm going to describe a job out loud — clean it up into clear notes I can send the customer or save for the file. Here it is: [dictate].

24. Compare two options

Compare [material/option A] vs [B] for a [job], for a customer who cares most about [price / durability / looks]. Give me a straight recommendation.

25. The "make this sound right" catch-all

Here's something I wrote for a customer. Make it sound professional but still like me, fix any typos, and keep it short.
