Research with Claude: answers, not tabs.

Research isn't collecting links — it's reaching a defensible answer. Claude searches the web, reads sources, and synthesizes across them; your job is directing it with the discipline that makes the output trustworthy.

01 Ask for the answer-shape, not the topic

Weak: "research payroll providers." Strong: "I need to choose a payroll provider for an 8-person company in North Dakota. Search current options, compare the top four on price, contractor support, and state tax filing, and recommend with reasoning. Cite sources for every factual claim." The answer-shape — compare, on these criteria, recommend, cite — is what turns search into research.

02 The source discipline

Demand citations, then click one. The load-bearing claim — the price, the deadline, the regulation — gets verified at its source before you act. One click per decision is the tax; pay it.
Ask about source quality: "Which of your sources is weakest, and what would you want to confirm elsewhere?" Claude answers this honestly — most people never ask.
Date-check anything volatile. Prices, policies, product features: "How current is this, and what might have changed since?" (This site exists because AI tools change weekly; assume your research subject does too.)

03 Synthesis: where Claude earns it

The upgrade over search engines isn't finding — it's holding many sources in mind at once. Use that: "Across everything you found, where do sources disagree? What's the strongest case on each side? What would settle it?" Disagreement-mapping is the highest-value research ask, because the disagreements are exactly where your decision risk lives.

The brief format

End every research session with: "Compress this into a one-page brief: the question, the answer, the confidence level, the evidence, what would change your mind." Briefs accumulate; conversations evaporate. A Project full of dated briefs is a research asset your competitors don't have.

04 Deep vs. quick — match the tool to the stakes

Quick factual checks: just ask, verify the one claim, move on. Real decisions: full answer-shape prompt, source discipline, disagreement map, brief. The failure mode in both directions is mismatch — deep-researching trivia, or coin-flipping decisions on one unverified summary. Stakes decide the mode.

Try it now

Take a real pending decision that needs facts. Run the full pattern: answer-shape prompt, one source verified, the disagreement map, one-page brief. Compare against how you'd have researched it Tuesday.
