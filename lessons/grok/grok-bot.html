Grok Bot: your team of always-on AI agents.

On August 11, 2026, SpaceXAI launched Grok Bot — and it isn’t another chatbot. It’s a team of AI agents, each with its own computer in the cloud, that sign into the tools you already use and keep working 24/7, finishing multi-step jobs and only coming back when something needs your approval. It’s an early beta, it’s built on Cursor, and — importantly — it’s locked behind SpaceXAI’s most expensive tiers. Here’s what it actually does, who can get it, and how to judge it honestly.

02 Why it’s different from a chatbot

A normal AI chat waits for a prompt, answers, and forgets. Grok Bot is built to be handed work like a coworker:

  • It has its own computer. Jobs don’t stall when you step away — the Bot keeps going in the cloud, and it can sign into apps, tools, and websites even ones with no clean API, clicking through them the way a person would.
  • You message it like a teammate. No workflows to build or name — you text it a task from your phone or desktop and it takes it from there, and you can pick the thread back up on either device.
  • You can run many Bots at once. Inside SpaceXAI, people run a “chief of staff” Bot that manages specialist Bots — one for the inbox, one for expenses, one for bug fixes — and the Bots message each other and coordinate in group chats, pulling you in only for judgment calls.
  • You show it how it’s done. Ask a Bot to follow along the next time you do a task; it watches, saves the steps as a routine, and runs it on its own next time.
  • It gets sharper over time. Bots keep context on how you like work done — your voice, your edge cases, when to ping you versus keep going.

If you’ve used Grok Automations, think of this as the next rung up: Automations run one saved job on a schedule or email trigger; a Bot is a standing teammate that works across everything, unprompted.

03 What people actually use it for

SpaceXAI built Grok Bot internally first, and the real examples are mundane in the best way — the multi-step busywork that usually eats a morning:

  • Sales: a Bot researches accounts overnight, scores contacts, and leaves an inbox of personalized email and LinkedIn drafts to approve; another updates the CRM from call transcripts.
  • Operations & finance: seating new hires, processing invoices that land in Gmail, keeping records clean.
  • Engineering: one Bot reproduces a bug in the product UI and files the ticket, then hands the fix to a debugging Bot.

The through-line: the work lands in the actual tool, where a human would have put it — the difference between “90% done” and actually finished.

The honest read: this is a genuine step up from scheduled automations — but mind the fine print. It’s early beta, and it’s gated behind SpaceXAI’s priciest plans (SuperGrok Heavy) plus Cursor’s Ultra and Teams Premium tiers — a regular Grok user can’t touch it yet. And “an agent with its own computer, signed into your inboxes and tools, working unsupervised” is exactly as powerful and as sensitive as it sounds: you’re granting a Bot standing access to your accounts. Start it on low-stakes work, watch the first runs, and read Grok and privacy before you hand over the keys.

How to approach it

  • Not on SuperGrok Heavy or a Cursor Ultra/Teams plan? This is a watch-and-wait — the wider rollout is tied to Grok 4.6 this week
  • If you have access: give your first Bot one narrow, low-stakes, repeatable job and review every run for a week before trusting it
  • Use the “show it how it’s done” feature — teaching by demonstration beats writing instructions
  • Never give a Bot standing access to an account you’d panic about it acting in unsupervised

This week’s challenge

Whether or not you can run Grok Bot yet, do the thought experiment: write down the one multi-step task you repeat most that lives across two or three apps (research → draft → log it somewhere). That exact shape — cross-app, repetitive, “90% done is not done” — is what agent teammates like Grok Bot are built for. Knowing your own is how you’ll judge whether any of them are worth paying for.
