Build a Website · Lesson 1 Free  ~10 min · No code

Your first AI-built website, starting from nothing.

You don't need to learn to code, and you don't need to commit to one AI. In about 20 minutes you can have a real, shareable one-page site live on the internet. This lesson covers the three ways to build with AI, which to start with today, and the exact prompt that turns a paragraph about your idea into a working page.

A promise: by the end you'll have published something real — not a tutorial sandbox, an actual URL you can send to someone. Multi-page sites, conversion copy, custom code, and your own domain come in Lessons 2 and 3. This one is about getting off zero.

The mental model

AI doesn't "build websites." It collapses the three hard parts so your only job is judgment.

Building a site used to mean three skills: deciding what it should say, generating the pages and design, and refining until it's right. AI shortcuts all three — you describe the idea, it produces a first draft, you steer with plain feedback. Your only job becomes "is this the right navigation bar," not "how do I make one." You're the editor; the AI is the builder.

The reframeDone and live beats perfect and unpublished. Get one honest page live today. You'll learn more from a real URL than from another hour of planning.

Step 02 · Build your site brief

The quality of an AI-built site is decided almost entirely by five things you decide up front. Pick one option in each row — watch your starter prompt assemble itself below. (Use your real answers when you do this for real; these are examples.)

1 · Purpose what's the page for?
2 · Audience who is it for, in one line?
3 · The one action what should a visitor do?
4 · Sections the blocks the page needs
5 · Tone three adjectives

That's the prompt that builds your page.

Notice the two lines doing the heavy lifting: "Use real, specific copy — no invented statistics, testimonials, or client logos" and "Ask me up to 3 questions before you build." Those turn a generic page into one that fits your situation — and keep you out of trouble.

Then refine in plain English: "The headline is too generic — name the exact problem in under 10 words." Vague notes get vague edits; specific notes get specific pages.

Delete anything invented. AI will happily add fake testimonials, logos, and confident-sounding stats to "fill" your page. Shipping a made-up "Trusted by 5,000 companies" is the fastest way to lose trust — and a real legal risk. Only publish claims that are true.

Step 03 · What every page must include

A site that looks nice but doesn't convert is a pretty dead end. Before you publish, check the page actually does its job:

  • A headline that says what it is and who it's for — a stranger should get it in five seconds.
  • One primary action, repeated — the same clear button near the top and again at the bottom.
  • A reason to trust you — a real result, credential, photo, or specific guarantee.
  • A way to reach you — an email or form for people who don't convert today.
  • Mobile that works — most visitors are on a phone. Open it on yours before sharing.
Get it live. Almost every prompt-to-site builder has a Publish button that gives you a free shareable URL on the spot. Click it — you now have a real website. Your own domain (yourname.com) is a Lesson 3 topic; don't let it block you from shipping the free URL today.

Your challenge: ship one real page today

Pick something you actually have — a side project, a service, an event, your résumé as a page. Then:

  1. Write your five answers (purpose, audience, one action, sections, tone).
  2. Run the starter prompt and answer the AI's questions.
  3. Do two refinement passes. Delete anything invented.
  4. Check it on your phone, then hit Publish.
  5. Send the live link to one person: "Is it obvious what this is and what to do?"
