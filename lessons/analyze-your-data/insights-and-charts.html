Insights & charts that tell the story.

Answers are raw material; a story is what moves people. This lesson goes from numbers to the right charts and a clear narrative you can put in front of your boss or your team — and how to tell a real insight from noise.

The mental model

A chart is an argument. The right one makes the point in a glance; the wrong one buries it.

Data doesn't persuade — a clear story does. The job is to find the one or two things that actually matter, pick the chart that makes each obvious, and wrap it in a sentence a busy person gets instantly. AI builds the charts; you decide what story they tell.

The reframeLead with the insight, not the data. Don't present six charts and hope. Say the one thing that matters, then show the single chart that proves it.

Step 01 · Pick the right chart

Each question wants a particular chart. Match the question to the chart that makes its point in a glance:

"How has revenue changed over 12 months?"
"Which five products sell the most?"
"What share of revenue comes from each region?"

Step 02 · Insight, or noise?

Not every wiggle in the data means something. Tap each finding — a real insight worth reporting, or noise?

Story promptFrom this data, give me the 2 most important findings for [audience], each as one plain sentence with the number that proves it. For each, tell me the single best chart type and why. Ignore noise — only things that would change a decision.
AI will find a "pattern" in pure noise if you ask it to. Anything based on tiny differences or a handful of data points isn't an insight — it's randomness. Insist on findings big enough to matter and ask "would this change a decision?"

Your challenge: tell one data story

  1. Pull the 2 findings that would actually change a decision.
  2. Pick the right chart for each and have AI build it.
  3. Write each as one plain sentence with its proof number.
  4. Cut everything else — present the story, not the data dump.
